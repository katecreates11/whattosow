# What To Sow — Copy Rewrite Pack

Surface-by-surface rewrite rules. Companion to `docs/tone-of-voice.md` (the voice)
and `docs/ANTI_PATTERNS.md` §10 (the smells). Use this when writing or reviewing
any user-facing string.

**The three ways copy fails here, in order of how often we ship them:**

1. **Dashboard-cold** — data without judgement ("Last frost: 19 Apr").
2. **AI-poetic** — images that carry no information ("the thirsty earth yearns").
3. **Try-hard whimsy** — charm applied like glitter ("your garden canvas awaits!").

**The calibration rule:** warmth comes from verbs, honesty and specificity — never
from adjectives. **One image per moment, maximum, and it must carry information.**
"The beds have been giving water to the sky all day" earns its place (it *is*
evaporation). "A small act of time travel" for frozen beans is right on the line —
lovely once, purple if it has neighbours. If an image is doing personality without
information, cut it. When in doubt, write the plain version; the plain version in
this voice is already warm.

---

## 1. Postcode Answer copy

- **Bad (cold):** "Success! Location found: Barnet. Last frost: 19 Apr. Growing
  season: 179 days."
- **Bad (try-hard):** "Ah, Barnet! A gardener's paradise where the frosts dance
  away each April…"
- **Better:** "Growing in Barnet, then — your last frost went on 19 April, and
  the whole season's open. Sow this week: French beans, beetroot, quick salads."
- **Why it works:** recognition ("then"), one *interpreted* fact (not echoed), and
  usefulness inside the same breath. The warmth is in "then" and "went", not in
  adjectives.
- **Avoid:** Success!, We found, your personalised results, exclamation marks,
  paradise/magical/journey, restating raw fields.
- **Ours:** "Growing in ___, then" · "your last frost went on ___" · "the whole
  season's open" · "about ten days left" · plain place names doing the charm.

## 2. Watering notes

- **Bad (cold):** "Watering recommended. Precipitation 0mm. Evapotranspiration
  high."
- **Bad (poetic):** "The thirsty earth yearns for the watering can's gentle kiss."
- **Better:** "The beds have been giving water to the sky all day. A slow, deep
  soak at the roots this evening — pots first, they're always thirstiest."
- **Why:** verdict in the first clause, one working image, then the instruction.
  A note, not a reading.
- **Avoid:** yearns/whispers/dances/kisses, hydration, moisture levels, two images
  in one note, "don't forget to…" (nagging register).
- **Ours:** "a proper drink" · "at the roots, never the leaves" · "the sky's
  taken this one" · "pots first" · "have the evening off".

## 3. Too late / worth waiting

- **Bad:** "Sweetcorn: too late to start from seed now." ×5 · "Unfortunately,
  you've missed the window."
- **Better:** "Worth waiting on: sweetcorn, courgettes and pumpkins *from seed* —
  they now need more weeks than the season has left. As young plants they're
  still fair game this week, or circle them for spring."
- **Why:** one sentence not five rows; "from seed" kills the contradiction with
  plug-planting; the *instead* is always attached; no blame words.
- **Avoid:** unfortunately, you've missed, expired, bare "too late" with no
  pivot, apologising for the calendar.
- **Ours:** "worth waiting on" · "their moment comes round again" · "circle them
  for spring" · "the season owes them more than it has".

## 4. Crop page verdicts

- **Bad (status):** "STATUS: OUT OF SEASON."
- **Bad (try-hard):** "Alas, dear gardener, the tomato ship has sailed."
- **Better:** "Too late to sow for this year — a plant started now would meet the
  autumn frosts still flowering. Already growing them? This page is yours: July
  is side-shoots and steady water."
- **Why:** verdict → reason (as a concrete image that *is* the explanation) →
  pivot. Three beats, never fewer than two.
- **Avoid:** alas, dear gardener, ship-has-sailed, status-register words
  (ACTIVE/EXPIRED/UNAVAILABLE), hedging ("it may possibly be somewhat late").
- **Ours:** "if you're quick" · "and that's fine — here's why" · "last chance —
  about ten days" · "not yet, and that's the good news: time to get the bed ready".

## 5. Affiliate disclosures

- **Bad (cold):** only the legal line, nothing human.
- **Bad (begging):** "Every click helps water our little dream! 🌱"
- **Better:** "If you buy through these links, a little goes towards a proper
  shed for the allotment — no ads, just the things we actually use, honestly
  told."
- **Why:** the shed is true and specific; "honestly told" is a promise the page
  then has to keep; no begging, no hiding.
- **Avoid:** emoji, support-our-journey, "at absolutely NO cost to you!!",
  burying the disclosure, over-apologising for existing.
- **Ours:** "towards the shed" · "the things we actually use" · "we earn a
  little" · keep any legally-required Amazon line, but never as the only line.

## 6. Buying advice

- **Bad:** "This premium, high-quality product is a game-changer for serious
  gardeners. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐"
- **Better (worth):** "Once the first truss has set, a weekly high-potash feed
  makes a real difference to yield — this is the one we use."
- **Better (skip):** "Skip seed tapes — four times the price, and thinning is
  part of how carrots work."
- **Why:** tied to the crop's real failure mode; ownership claimed only when
  true; every skip makes the worths believable.
- **Avoid:** game-changer, must-have, premium, unbeatable, essential-for-every-
  gardener, unearned stars, "you NEED", price-shaming the reader.
- **Ours:** "earns its place" · "the one we use" (only if Kate owns it) · "worth
  it the first year you don't lose a row" · "the free version: …".

## 7. Navigation labels

- **Bad:** Resources · Tools · Features · Solutions · Get Started · Hub.
- **Better:** Sow · Grow · Harvest · Guides · Blog · My garden.
- **Why:** navigation is wayfinding — the one place clarity beats charm, every
  time. The gardener's own verbs are already the perfect labels.
- **Avoid:** marketing nouns, cleverness in nav, renaming known things
  ("The Potting Bench" for the blog — no).
- **Ours:** single craft words; "Still time to sow" is the allowed exception
  because it's a real page making a real promise.

## 8. Footer links

- **Bad:** "Support this tool →" · three columns of every route · "©2026 All
  rights reserved" energy.
- **Better:** "Towards the shed →" · five curated doors · "Frost dates worked
  from Met Office records." · the seasonal whisper ("It's summer — keep sowing,
  keep watering.")
- **Why:** "tool" is the wrong self-image; the shed is the support story and
  it's ours. Provenance lines are trust, not smallprint.
- **Avoid:** tool/platform/app as self-description, link farms, orphan links
  that couldn't earn one sentence of why-you'd-click.
- **Ours:** the whisper · "made on an allotment in ___" if we ever want a
  maker's mark · plain data credits.

## 9. Empty states

- **Bad:** "No data available." · "0 results found." · "Your garden canvas
  awaits its first masterpiece!"
- **Better:** "Nothing to sow this week on the UK average — your own postcode
  may differ." · "Nothing growing here yet — every good garden started exactly
  this way." · (deep winter list) "Six things are sowable in January. The rest
  are waiting with us."
- **Why:** state the fact, face forward, include ourselves. An empty bed is a
  normal part of gardening — the copy should already know that.
- **Avoid:** No data, 0 results, exclamation-mark enthusiasm, canvas/masterpiece/
  journey/adventure, blaming the season.
- **Ours:** "yet" · "the quiet weeks" · "check back as the season turns" · "waiting
  with us" · an illustration where one exists (RakedBed, Dot).

## 10. Error states

- **Bad:** "Error 500: Something went wrong." · "Oops! Our bad! 😅" · "Invalid
  input."
- **Better (postcode fails):** "We couldn't find that postcode — worth checking
  a letter? It happens to us weekly."
- **Better (weather API down):** "The weather isn't answering just now — your
  sowing dates below still stand; they're worked from your frost pattern, not
  today's forecast."
- **Why:** say what failed, say what still works, include ourselves in the
  fallibility. Never a dead end: every error names the part of the page that's
  still good.
- **Avoid:** Oops, emoji, error codes at the reader, "invalid" (blames them),
  apology theatre ("we're SO sorry!!"), silent failure (worse than any copy).
- **Ours:** "worth checking ___?" · "still stands" · "try again in a minute —
  about a kettle's length" · "it happens to us weekly".

---

**The final test, for any string on any surface:** read it aloud at the allotment
gate to someone holding a trug. If you'd be embarrassed — it's try-hard. If
they'd ask "so what do I do?" — it's cold. If they'd nod and get on with it —
ship it.
