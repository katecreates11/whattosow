## Dreamt connections

**1. Last week's biggest open question answered itself, and the answer was
better than any of the guesses.** The dream held "a growing pile suppresses
decisions" as a genuine, low-confidence hypothesis. It was wrong. Kate
wasn't avoiding the board because it was long — she was building the fix
for a *different* stuck-pipeline problem, then came back and cleared the
whole thing in one sitting. The lesson isn't about pile size at all: it's
that a quiet board week is a poor signal on its own, and worth reading
against what else Kate might be building before drawing a taste conclusion
from it.

**2. The backlog clearing just recreated the exact shape of problem it grew
out of, from the other end.** The Night Gardener can now only carry one PR
at a time by its own new rule, and there are seven approved, build-ready
cards waiting. PR #2 went stale sitting open for two weeks with nobody
building against it; the same clock is now running on cards 9 (National
Allotments Week, two days from its own week) and 18 (giant-veg tool, one
day from Worcester's judging). The fix for "PRs get stuck once open" didn't
touch "approved cards queue for weeks before a PR even opens" — a
genuinely different bottleneck, wearing the same clothes.

**3. Dreamt scope-additions are no longer a hopeful pattern — they're the
best-performing move in the whole portfolio.** Five for five now (10, 11,
12, 16, 20), a cleaner record than the Forager's own fresh pitches. The
common thread: none of them ask for a new page or a new build, just a
sharper brief on something already approved and still unbuilt — cheap to
say yes to, because saying yes doesn't add anything to the queue.

**4. Three routines went quiet in the same week Kate was writing repair
code.** Forager, Groundskeeper and the SEO Watchdog all stopped producing
reports in roughly the same window PR #2 sat rotting. That's either a real
scheduling fault worth someone's direct attention, or — following
connection #1 — simply Kate's own bandwidth being spent somewhere else and
nothing to do with the schedules at all. Flagged, not concluded.

## What if

**What if National Allotments Week gets written as it happens, not after
the fact?** Card 9 is very unlikely to be built and live by 10 August. A
diary piece written mid-week, dated honestly, would read truer than one
that pretends to have been ready on day one — and it's the kind of thing
only a real plot, not a template, can do.

**What if the queue itself gets a build order by urgency instead of board
order?** Nothing in the Night Gardener's spec currently ranks the seven
waiting cards by how soon their hook expires. A one-line addition — build
the most time-sensitive approved card first, not the oldest or the
highest-scored — would have caught both card 9 and card 18 automatically
this week.
