# Dream — 14 November 2026 (week 18)

## Status, before anything else

96 days since the last Potting Bench decision (card 21, 10 August) — the
fourteenth silent week running. PR #3 (the Glut-o-meter) at 101 days,
still zero reviews, zero human comments. But on 11 November, `main` moved
for the first time in 23 days — and the fix matched the SEO Watchdog's

Three things worth saying:

**One — `main` moved on 11 November, and the shape of what changed is the
most useful single fact this memory has found all quarter.** Commit
`3060827` ("Batch A") fixed, in this exact order: the orphaned
`/grow-your-christmas-dinner` page (linked from the homepage, `/sow`, and
the autumn-winter guide); the homepage's duplicate `<h1>`; `/kit`'s wrong
canonical; the missing `/privacy` sitemap entry; and `/admin` left open
in robots. Those are FIX NOW items #1 through #5 from the SEO Watchdog's
report the day before — in the report's own priority order. Whether Kate
opened that branch directly or a session of hers found the same five
things independently, the shape of what got fixed is a checklist, not a
conversation: five small, low-judgement, file-and-line fixes, no decision
required beyond "yes, do this." Compare that to the two channels this
memory has been testing for weeks with nothing to show: PR #3 (a review
request) and eighteen Gmail drafts (two formats, both prose). The thing
that worked wasn't either of those.

**Two — last week's framing needs a correction, and it's a happier one
than a miss usually is.** The 7 November dream said "nothing in this
system has reached you in three months through any channel it
controls." That was true of the channels it was testing — and still is:
the Bench, PR #3 and all eighteen Gmail drafts remain completely silent.
But four days later, something else reached Kate, and she acted inside a
day. The correct diagnosis isn't "Kate isn't engaging" — it's "Kate isn't
engaging with *these specific channels*, but is still actively working on
the site on her own terms." That's a better starting point for next
steps than resignation would have been.

**Three — the Forager's 8 November run is the second clean one in a row,
and this time it was pointed at the fix on purpose.** It branched from
`dreams/2026-11-07`, named card 85 as the reason, produced four pitches
(a December gift guide, forcing amaryllis/paperwhites, a bulb-forcing
countdown feature, a skeptical Black Friday piece), zero duplicates, and
avoided every one of the seventeen-ish families already sitting on the
board. Two clean runs out of two since this was first spotted (1 and 8
November) is a real pattern, even though the causal story is muddied by
the fact the first of the two wasn't told to do this at all — see the
self-score below.

Separately: the Groundskeeper's 9 November report is a genuinely careful
one — it distinguishes a Cloudflare bot-wall (Suttons, Thompson & Morgan,

## Dreamt connections

**1. The three-month mystery of "why doesn't anything land" just got its
first real clue, and it points away from the crew's own channels, not
toward a better version of them.** Eighteen Gmail drafts in two formats:
nothing. Ninety-four days of PR #3 sitting open for review: nothing. One
ranked SEO checklist: fixed within a day, in order. The common thread
isn't urgency or length — it's that a checklist needs no judgement call,
and a PR review or an email both implicitly ask for one. Worth testing
deliberately rather than treated as settled on n=1 — see card 91.

**2. The "point the Forager at the right branch" fix and the "give Kate a
checklist, not a conversation" finding are the same shape of lesson,
twice in two weeks.** Card 85 worked (with the caveat above) because it
turned a vague ask ("use good judgement about which branch") into a
specific, checkable instruction. The SEO Watchdog's FIX NOW list worked
for the same reason, on a much bigger stage. Neither fix required asking
anyone to decide something subjective — both just needed someone to say
"yes, do the five things on this list."

**3. Rhubarb-forcing (49) and bulb-forcing (87) are the same gardening
trick — denying a dormant thing light or warmth out of its normal season
— applied to a vegetable and a flower, in the same six weeks of the
calendar.** Neither pitch mentions the other. This is a cheap, honest
connection in the site's own closing-the-loop voice, not a new page —
see card 90.

**4. The site's single most time-sensitive SEO finding of the whole
autumn (the orphaned Christmas-dinner page) is now fixed, three days
before this dream, and nobody in this memory's chain asked for it
directly — it just happened to be first on a list.** That's a small but
real piece of evidence that ranking FIX NOW items by impact, as the SEO
Watchdog already does, is doing real work even when nothing ever gets
merged through the branch it's written on.

## What if

**What if PR #3 got rewritten as a checklist instead of a review ask?**
Not "please review this feature" but a flat list of exactly what merging
it changes on the live site, in the same five-line shape that worked on
11 November. This memory can't test this itself — it doesn't touch
code or PRs — but it's the single most concrete, falsifiable idea this
dream has produced in eighteen weeks, and it costs nothing to try. See
card 91.

**What if the next Groundskeeper run tries a real browser instead of a
plain fetch on just the six hand-picked Suttons URLs, rather than the
whole merchant?** This week's report already narrowed the ask correctly
— six specific product links, not sixty-six search queries — which makes
it the cheapest version of this experiment that's been proposed yet.
