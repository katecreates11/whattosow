# The SEO Watchdog — weekly SEO health

**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Scope:** on-page/technical audit (repo + live site), rough indexing check (`site:` search — no Search Console access yet), seasonal opportunities.
**This report only recommends. No code was changed, no branch was merged, nothing was deployed.**

## FIX NOW

Ranked by how many pages/how much visibility each touches.

1. **All 5 crop-playbook titles are 76–86 characters — truncated in Google, cutting off the brand name on the site's highest-value pages.**
   `src/app/crops/[slug]/page.tsx:427-429` builds titles like *"How to Grow Tomatoes in the UK — When to Plant, Care & Problems | What To Sow"* for the 5 playbook crops (tomatoes 77, carrots 76, courgettes 79, maincrop potatoes 86, runner beans 81 chars). Google truncates around ~60 chars, so every one of these loses "Care & Problems | What To Sow" in the SERP. Fix: shorten to `"${name} in the UK — When to Plant | What To Sow"` (~50 chars); move "Care & Problems" into the description, which has room.

2. **Nearly every `/guides/*` page (≈30 of 33) and all 16 companion-planting topic pages have titles over 60 chars and descriptions over 160 chars — a systemic pattern, not stray pages.**
   The template is `"{Title} (UK Guide) | What To Sow"` for titles (66–79 chars sampled) and full-sentence descriptions (162–215 chars sampled, e.g. `guides/watering-while-away/page.tsx` at 214, `guides/growing-root-vegetables/page.tsx` at 213). Same shape in `src/data/companion-topics.ts` (descriptions 181–215 chars across all 16 topics). Fix once per family: drop the `(UK Guide)` parenthetical or the trailing brand suffix on titles; cap descriptions at ~155 chars written as a snippet, not a sentence. Because it's data-driven for the companion topics, one pass through `companion-topics.ts` fixes all 16 at once.

3. **Bug: the 51 `/sow-in/[city]` page titles and descriptions go stale after every month-end, and nobody would notice until it shows up in search.**
   `src/app/sow-in/[city]/page.tsx:39-48` computes `monthName`/`year` from `new Date()` inside `generateMetadata`, but the route has **no `revalidate` export** (unlike `crops/[slug]/page.tsx:408`, which has `revalidate = 86400`). Since these are statically generated at build time, all 51 titles/descriptions freeze at whichever month the site was last deployed in — e.g. "What to Sow in Leicester — July 2026 Sowing Guide" will still say July in September until the next deploy. Given the "batch everything, one deploy at a time" cadence, this could sit stale for weeks. Fix: add `export const revalidate = 86400;` to that route (matching the crop-page pattern).

4. **17 of the 24 auto-generated monthly blog posts are publicly live right now with zero crawl path — a bigger problem than the planned removal fixes.**
   `src/data/blog-posts.ts` gates `getPublishedPosts()` (feeds the sitemap and the `/blog` index) by `publishDate <= now`, so only 7 of 24 auto-posts (through July 2026) show up anywhere a reader or crawler can click. But `generateStaticParams()` in `src/app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx:24-26` builds **all 24, unfiltered**, and `getPostBySlug()` has no date check at all — so the 17 future-dated posts (Aug 2026–Dec 2027) are statically built and served normally at their real URLs today, just invisible from the sitemap, the blog index, and prev/next nav. That's a live, publicly reachable page with no internal or sitemap path to it at all — worse than a normal orphan. Separate from the CUT_LIST §4 decision on whether these posts should exist long-term (⛔ needs Kate): right now, fix the bug either by having `getPostBySlug`/`generateStaticParams` respect the publish-date gate (so unpublished slugs 404 until their date) or by exposing a "coming next month" teaser link so they're at least discoverable on purpose.

5. **The entire `/sow-in` cluster (52 pages: the index + 51 cities) has zero inbound links from the rest of the site — an orphan family by design, not accident.**
   `src/app/sow-in/page.tsx` does link out to all 51 cities, and each city links to a few nearby cities — so the cluster is internally well-woven. But nothing outside `/sow-in/*` links into it: no Header link, no homepage link, and the Footer actively excludes it (`src/app/search-console-readiness.test.ts:114-115` asserts the footer must **not** contain `href="/sow-in"`). Combined with the thin-content flag already on these pages (CUT_LIST §4), a 52-page cluster with no inbound link equity is a plausible reason Google is slow to rank them. This is a product decision (CUT_LIST gates any change to these pages behind Kate's sign-off, ⛔) — flagging for that conversation rather than recommending a specific fix here.

6. **Duplicate meta descriptions across years for the same auto-generated month.** `src/data/blog-posts.ts`'s `generateDescription()` builds `` `What to sow in ${monthName} in the UK — ${total} crops to start this month...` `` from the same `crops.ts` dataset regardless of year — so "January 2026" and "January 2027" (and every repeated month across the two years) get byte-identical descriptions on two different indexable URLs. A genuine duplicate-content signal, separate from the CUT_LIST plan to retire these posts.

7. **`crops/page.tsx` and `sow/page.tsx` — the two highest-priority index pages in the sitemap (priority 0.9 each) — have no structured data at all.** Every major templated family (crop pages, variety pages, city pages, companion topics, guides, blog) carries JSON-LD; these two flagship index pages don't. Add `ItemList`/`CollectionPage` schema to both. (`harvest/page.tsx`, `harvest-planner/page.tsx`, `frost-map/page.tsx`, `grow/page.tsx` are lower-priority versions of the same gap.)

8. **`/still-time` and `/grow-your-christmas-dinner` are single, fully isolated pages — zero inbound links from anywhere else on the site**, reachable only via the sitemap. Cheap to fix: one contextual link each (e.g. `/still-time` from `/calendar` or the homepage list; `/grow-your-christmas-dinner` from `/harvest` or a relevant guide).

9. **Homepage renders two literal `<h1>` elements in server HTML** (`src/app/page.tsx:265` mobile, `:295` desktop — same copy, mutually exclusive via Tailwind `sm:hidden`/`hidden sm:flex`). Real crawl risk is low since Googlebot renders CSS, but any HTML-only auditor will flag "multiple h1." Cheap fix: one `<h1>` with responsive text-size classes instead of two duplicated elements. (Worth knowing separately: `layout.tsx:95` also puts an `<h1>` inside a sitewide `<noscript>` block — harmless, since real crawlers execute JS and ignore noscript, but it means a no-JS view of *every* page technically has 2–3 h1 tags.)

10. **54 files still carry a `keywords:` meta array**, including `layout.tsx`, every guide, every companion topic, and the crop/city/month templates. Per `docs/CUT_LIST.md`, Google has ignored this tag since 2009 — harmless but cargo-cult debt. A batch removal across all 54 files would be a clean, low-risk cleanup whenever there's a spare hour; no rush, but worth doing before more pages copy the pattern.

**Not flagged as new, only reconfirmed:** the 181 variety pages (~100–160 words of genuinely unique text per page — the `personality` field plus 1–2 recipe blurbs) and the 51 city pages (~40–70 words unique per page, mostly the `growingNotes` field) are both thin by conventional standards, matching CUT_LIST's existing "on watch" flag. CUT_LIST already gates any pruning decision on Search Console data (⛔), so no new action here — just confirming the thin-content risk is real, and the city pages read thinner than the varieties.

## INDEXING

*Approximate — done via `site:whattosow.co.uk` web search, not Search Console. Under-reporting is common; treat every "not found" as "no evidence of indexing" rather than "confirmed unindexed." One useful technical fact from the repo: `robots.ts` allows Googlebot/Bingbot unrestricted, and every page below is already present in `sitemap.ts` — so there's no crawl-blocker causing any of this; it reads as "needs indexing requested or more time," which Search Console access would confirm outright.*

**Confirmed showing up in search:** homepage, `/sow/february`, several crop pages (rocket, peas, leeks, broccoli, brussels-sprouts, cauliflower, squash, onion-sets), `/guides/companion-planting` hub, `/guides/growing-brassicas`, `/guides/allotment-essentials`, `/calendar`, `/sow-in/leicester` (confirms the route type *can* rank), one blog post.

**No evidence of indexing — worth a manual "request indexing" pass once Search Console access exists, highest priority first:**

- **`/blight-watch` and `/guides/tomato-blight`** — top priority. It's peak blight season (see Opportunities below), the site has a genuine edge here (a live postcode blight tool *plus* a guide, more than most competitors), and neither page showed up in any search variant tried.
- **`/guides/dealing-with-the-glut`** — directly on-topic for a live seasonal query (courgette glut, see below) and invisible in search.
- **`/guides/sow-now-eat-all-winter`** (published 6 days ago) and **`/guides/watering-while-away`** — no evidence of indexing; the first is simply too new, worth a request-indexing nudge rather than waiting.
- **`/crops` and `/sow` root index pages** — never appeared as their own result, only child pages did. Same two pages flagged above for missing schema (#7) — worth fixing both issues together.
- **`/sow-in/london`, `/sow-in/manchester`, `/sow-in/durham`** — none appeared directly despite `/sow-in/leicester` being indexed; London and Manchester are the highest-value city pages on the site, so this is worth a specific check even allowing for `site:` under-reporting.
- **`/crops/tomatoes`, `/crops/broad-beans`** — surprisingly invisible while smaller crops (squash, onion-sets) showed up; likely a display-cap artefact, but worth requesting indexing given these are two of the highest-traffic potential crop pages.
- **Variety pages and companion-topic sub-pages** (e.g. `/crops/tomatoes/sungold`, `/guides/companion-planting/companion-plants-for-tomatoes`) — no evidence of indexing in any search tried; consistent with these being deep, thinly-linked pages.

**One incidental flag, unrelated to the brief:** `whattosow.com` (a `.com`, not the site's `.co.uk`) surfaced as a top/near-top result in several unrelated searches, sometimes ranking alongside whattosow.co.uk. Worth a five-minute look at who owns that domain and whether it's causing brand confusion — not a Watchdog action item, just noting it landed in the search results.

## OPPORTUNITIES

Late July/UK, evidence-based only:

1. **Hosepipe bans / watering restrictions — strong current news, zero site coverage.** Multiple outlets (BBC, ITV News, Gardens Illustrated) report 8+ million UK households under Temporary Use Bans as of mid-July 2026 (South East Water, Southern Water, Anglian Water, South West Water, Cambridge Water, Affinity Water all named). `site:whattosow.co.uk hosepipe ban` returned zero results. The existing `/guides/watering-while-away` is about holiday watering — a different angle. **Recommend:** a piece on which watering methods stay legal under a ban (watering can, water butt, greywater) and which veg to prioritise when water's rationed. Real, current, and unaddressed.

2. **Courgette/veg glut — active seasonal query right now, own page invisible.** Multiple UK gardening/lifestyle sites are actively publishing "what to do with a courgette glut" content this week — the classic late-July peak-harvest query. `/guides/dealing-with-the-glut` is exactly on-topic but didn't surface in any search, direct or combined with "courgette glut." **Recommend:** request indexing (see above) and check that the courgette crop page links to it — improving internal links plus the indexing request should help more than either alone.

3. **Tomato blight — peak season, real product/content advantage, but invisible in search.** Several UK sites confirm July–August is peak blight-risk search season, noting blight can destroy an outdoor crop in 7–10 days. What To Sow already has more than most competitors here (a live postcode `/blight-watch` tool *and* a `/guides/tomato-blight` guide) — but neither page turned up in search. **Recommend:** this is the single highest-value indexing-request candidate this week (see INDEXING above) — genuine urgency, genuine differentiation, apparently zero current visibility.

4. **"What to sow in August" — the next competitive seasonal query, timing is imminent.** Suttons, Garden Organic, BBC Gardener's World, Dobies, GrowVeg and others all run active August-sowing content — a recurring, high-competition query that becomes relevant literally next week. The site's own `/sow/august` (parallel to the indexed `/sow/february`) didn't surface in any search. **Recommend:** request indexing ahead of the month turning over, so it has a head start rather than catching up in August.
