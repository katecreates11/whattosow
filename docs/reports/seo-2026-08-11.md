## FIX NOW

Ordered by impact. "Affects N pages" means one template change fixes all of them at once.

### 1. The entire `/sow-in` cluster — 52 pages — has zero internal links from anywhere on the site
`/sow-in` (index) and all 51 `/sow-in/[city]` pages are reachable only by direct URL, sitemap crawl, or search. Grepped every nav, footer, homepage block, and content page — nothing links in. `search-console-readiness.test.ts` even has a test asserting the footer must *not* link `/sow-in`, so this looks deliberate rather than accidental, but it's actively working against these pages: they carry `priority: 0.7-0.8` and `changeFrequency: "weekly"` in `sitemap.ts`, claiming importance the site's own navigation doesn't back up. Internal links are one of the strongest indexing signals you have and this cluster gets none.
**Fix:** add one real link — e.g. "or browse by city" next to the postcode tool on the homepage, or a link from `/sow-in` into the header's location/more menu. One link in, from a page Google already crawls often, fixes all 52 at once.

### 2. `/still-time` and `/grow-your-christmas-dinner` are also orphans
Same problem, smaller scale. Both are in the sitemap at `priority: 0.8`, both are indexable, neither has a single inbound link anywhere in `src/` outside their own file and `sitemap.ts`. `/still-time` is flagged in `docs/CUT_LIST.md` for a redirect-into-`/sow` treatment eventually — until that ships, it's an indexable page nobody can click to. `/grow-your-christmas-dinner` has no such removal note; it's most likely just missing a home in the nav or a seasonal homepage mention.
**Fix:** link both from somewhere real — a homepage seasonal band, footer, or a contextual link from a relevant guide.

### 3. `keywords` meta arrays are still on ~177 generated pages + 43 static pages + the root layout
`docs/CUT_LIST.md` already says to stop adding these — Google has ignored `keywords` since 2009. They're not actively harmful, but they're on almost every template: `crops/[slug]` (47 pages), `sow-in/[city]` (50), `sow/[month]` (12), `blog/[slug]` (51, both auto and editorial posts), `guides/companion-planting/[topic]` (17), plus 43 static pages and `layout.tsx` itself. A handful of pages (`crops/[slug]/[variety]`, `crops`, `grow`, `harvest`, `sow`, `privacy`, `us`, `my-plot`) have already had theirs removed, so this looks like a partial cleanup that stalled.
**Fix:** since this is genuinely mechanical (delete a field, no content change), it's a good one-shot cleanup pass across all listed files.

### 4. `/kit` — canonical points at the wrong page
`src/app/kit/page.tsx`: `alternates.canonical` and `openGraph.url` are both set to `/guides` instead of `/kit`. The page is currently noindexed, so it isn't costing anything today, but the canonical is simply wrong and should be fixed regardless — it'll matter the moment `/kit` goes live.

### 5. Titles and descriptions run long across most of `/guides/*`, all 17 companion-planting topic pages, and 20 of 27 editorial blog posts
Google truncates titles past ~55-60 characters and description snippets past ~155-160. This isn't a handful of stragglers — it's close to the default across two whole content types:
- **Titles >60 chars:** the large majority of the 33 static `/guides/*` pages (e.g. `growing-brassicas` at 78 chars, `growing-root-vegetables` at 79), all 17 `/guides/companion-planting/[topic]` pages (65-76 chars), the 5 crop pages with a playbook (`tomatoes`, `carrots`, `courgettes`, `maincrop-potatoes`, `runner-beans` — 73-88 chars), and 20/27 editorial blog posts (worst: 85 chars).
- **Descriptions >160 chars:** the same guide pages (worst: `autumn-planting-garlic-onions` at 233 chars), the same 17 companion-planting topics (181-215 chars), and 26/27 editorial posts (worst: 274 chars).

This reads like a single authoring habit — writing full, natural sentences without checking character count — repeated across every hand-written page type. It doesn't break anything, but it means Google is rewriting or clipping the SERP snippet on most of the site's highest-value content pages instead of showing what was actually written.
**Fix:** no need to rewrite everything at once — start with the highest-traffic guides and the companion-planting topics (smaller, self-contained set of 17), trim to 55-60 / 150-155 chars.

### 6. `/privacy` is a real, indexable, footer-linked page missing from `sitemap.ts`
It has full metadata, a correct canonical, and no noindex — every other page.tsx that's absent from the sitemap has an explicit `noindex` (`bed-planner`, `bench`, `kit`, `lucky-dip`, `my-garden`, `my-plot`, `pins`, `print`, `products`). `/privacy` is the one exception; it looks like a plain oversight.
**Fix:** add it to `sitemap.ts`.

### 7. `/admin/photos` has no metadata export and isn't blocked by robots.txt
Unlike its sibling internal tool `/bench` (which sets `noindex`), `/admin/photos` has no `metadata` export at all, so it silently inherits the homepage's title/description from the root layout. `robots.txt` only disallows named AI-training bots, not `/admin` generally, so nothing stops a normal crawler indexing this page with a duplicate homepage title if it's ever discovered. Low risk today (no inbound links either) but a one-line fix.
**Fix:** add `export const metadata = { robots: { index: false, follow: false } }`, matching `/bench`.

### 8. `/sow-in/[city]` titles bake in the current month/year at build time with no revalidation
The title template is `` `What to Sow in ${city.name} — ${monthName} ${year} Sowing Guide` ``, computed at static build time. `crops/[slug]` has an explicit `revalidate` export for exactly this kind of freshness; `sow-in/[city]` doesn't. Combined with `sitemap.ts` claiming `changeFrequency: "weekly"` for these pages, a title reading "— August 2026" could sit stale in search results for months after the next deploy passes.
**Fix:** add a `revalidate` export (or drop the month/year from the title, which also shortens it — see #5's cousin issue on this template, titles are otherwise a healthy 46-56 chars).

### 9. `/guides/tomato-blight` and `/blight-watch` compete for the same query
Both pages market "live blight risk by postcode" in their title and description and both render the same `<BlightRisk>` component. Not a bug exactly, but two pages chasing one intent split ranking signal instead of combining it. Worth a look at whether one should canonically own "blight risk" and the other differentiate (e.g. `/guides/tomato-blight` stays prevention/identification content, `/blight-watch` stays the live tool) more clearly in title/H1.

### 10. Structured data: seven long-form guides carry FAQ schema but no Article schema
`watering`, `pests`, `soil`, `crop-rotation`, `growing-fruit`, `seed-starting`, `beginners` — all have substantial editorial content (8-12 sections each, verified) and already have `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList`, but skip the `Article` schema that 20 sibling guides carry. Low-risk, mechanical addition matching the existing pattern (see e.g. `sow-now-eat-all-winter` or `dealing-with-the-glut` for the shape to copy).

Also:
- **`crops/[slug]/[variety]`** (181 pages) — only `BreadcrumbList`. These have real editorial voice and already set `openGraph.type: "article"` in metadata that the JSON-LD doesn't match; add `Article`.
- **44 of 47 crop pages** get no `FAQPage` schema because `FAQPage` is conditional on a "playbook" entry, and only 3 crops (`tomatoes`, `carrots`, `courgettes`) — sorry, the agent's fuller pass found 5 (`tomatoes`, `carrots`, `courgettes`, `maincrop-potatoes`, `runner-beans`) — have one. Not a bug, since there's no orphaned FAQ content without matching schema, but worth knowing the template supports more than the data currently uses.
- **`frost-map`** — zero JSON-LD of any kind, not even `BreadcrumbList`. Substantive tool/content page; worth at least matching the site-wide `BreadcrumbList` pattern.

### 11. `/sow-in/[city]` pages are thin — concrete word counts, not a guess
Measured the actual unique, hand-written text per city page (excluding shared template sentences and computed frost-date data): **26-47 words per page, averaging ~35 words.** Everything else — the frost-date table, the "crops to sow this month" list, most of the surrounding sentences — is the same template with the city name and a computed date substituted in. Example: Aberdeen and Bristol's pages differ only in one ~35-40 word paragraph; every heading and surrounding sentence is byte-identical apart from the name. This is close to the textbook definition of a thin doorway page, and it confirms `docs/CUT_LIST.md`'s existing instinct to hold this template for Search Console data and prune to the ~10 biggest cities if it isn't indexing. No code change recommended here — this is evidence for the decision already queued, not a new one.

By contrast, the **181 variety pages are only borderline** (52-137 words, avg ~74, but genuinely unique voice per page — not templated) and the **17 companion-planting topic pages are substantial** (298-494 words, avg ~414, real differentiated content). Don't lump these three page types together when deciding what to prune — only the city pages show the doorway-page pattern.

## OPPORTUNITIES

Evidence-based, current UK gardening season (mid-August).

1. **End-of-season tomato ripening / green tomatoes.** Real search volume — multiple dedicated articles on this right now (windowsill ripening, cardboard-drawer method, pinching the growing tip in late August, green tomato chutney). The `tomatoes` crop page's tip field covers feeding/pinching side shoots but nothing about the end-of-season ripening push; `guides/tomato-blight` only mentions ripening green fruit in a blight-emergency context. A short addition to the tomatoes crop page (or a seasonal note) would cover a real August query the site currently only touches sideways.

2. **Sweetcorn harvest readiness.** "How do I know when sweetcorn is ready" is a recurring query this time of year (tassel/silk colour test + kernel test). The `sweetcorn` crop entry's tip field covers block-planting for pollination but says nothing about telling ripeness — a one-line addition (silks turn dark brown → check a kernel, milky not watery or paste-like) would close a small, genuine gap.

3. **Raspberry pruning: summer- vs autumn-fruiting distinction.** `guides/growing-fruit`'s FAQ correctly covers autumn-fruiting types ("cut everything down in February") but doesn't clearly separate that from summer-fruiting canes (remove only the canes that fruited, tie in new green canes) — which is the distinction most competing UK content leads with. Worth expanding the existing FAQ answer rather than adding a new page.

No recommendation made without a page or gap to point at — these three are the ones with real query evidence behind them this week.
