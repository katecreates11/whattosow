## FIX NOW

### 1. `/privacy` is indexable but missing from the sitemap
`src/app/privacy/page.tsx` has a real title, description and no `noindex` — it's meant to be found —
but it's absent from `src/app/sitemap.ts`. It does have two internal links (footer), so it isn't a
pure orphan, but it gets none of the sitemap's "please index this" signal. **Fix:** add one entry to
the `sitemap.ts` array, e.g.
```ts
{ url: `${baseUrl}/privacy`, lastModified: new Date("2026-04-02"), changeFrequency: "yearly", priority: 0.3 },
```

### 2. Two sitemap pages have zero internal links anywhere on the site
Checked every `.tsx` file under `src/app` and `src/components` for a link to each sitemap URL.
Two indexable, decent-priority (0.7–0.8) pages came back with **no internal links at all** — not nav,
not footer, not a single guide or blog mention:

- **`/grow-your-christmas-dinner`** (sitemap priority 0.8) — genuinely worth fixing now. It's a
  countdown-format page and September is exactly when it starts being relevant again. It also didn't
  surface for a direct `"whattosow.co.uk" grow your christmas dinner` search, while several
  competitors did (Suttons, Kitchen Garden, Two Wests & Elliott) — consistent with, though not proof
  of, weak indexing from having no link equity at all. Natural link points: `/guides/growing-brassicas`
  (sprouts), `/guides/preparing-your-plot-for-winter`, `/guides/autumn-planting-garlic-onions`, and a
  seasonal nudge from the homepage or footer as the countdown gets meaningful.
- **`/still-time`** (sitemap priority 0.8, `changeFrequency: daily`) — also zero internal links, but
  `docs/CUT_LIST.md` already calls this one out for retirement ("demote to a redirect into `/sow`'s
  amber tags once the listings redesign ships"). Don't spend new links on a page that's already
  scheduled to go — this finding is really confirmation that the redirect is overdue: right now the
  page does neither job. Worth prioritising that already-approved cut over anything else here.

(`/us` looked like a third candidate on a plain `href="/us"` grep, but it's linked from the footer as
`/us?source=footer` — false alarm, not an orphan.)

### 3. Batch: 29 page titles run past ~60 characters, almost all in `/guides`
Google typically truncates SERP titles around 55–60 characters. 28 of the 29 overlong titles are
guide pages, all following the same `"[Long descriptive title] (UK) | What To Sow"` pattern — this
reads like one batch, so it's a single pass to fix, not 29 separate edits. Worst offenders:

| Length | Page | Current title |
|---|---|---|
| 79 | `/guides/growing-root-vegetables` | Growing Root Vegetables — Carrots, Beetroot, Parsnips & More (UK) \| What To Sow |
| 78 | `/guides/growing-squash-pumpkins-courgettes` | Growing Squash, Pumpkins & Courgettes — The Cucurbit Family (UK) \| What To Sow |
| 78 | `/guides/growing-brassicas` | Growing Brassicas — Cabbage, Kale, Broccoli & Sprouts (UK Guide) \| What To Sow |
| 76 | `/guides/tomato-blight` | Tomato & Potato Blight — Live Hutton Criteria Risk by Postcode \| What To Sow |
| 75 | `/guides/growing-onions-garlic-leeks` | Growing Onions, Garlic & Leeks — The Allium Family (UK Guide) \| What To Sow |

Full list of 29 is in the branch's working notes if useful, but the fix is the same everywhere: drop
either the "(UK Guide)" qualifier or the " | What To Sow" suffix — not both, one is enough — to bring
each under ~60 characters. e.g. `Growing Root Vegetables UK — Carrots, Beetroot & Parsnips`.

### 4. Batch: 26 meta descriptions run past ~160 characters, same `/guides` cluster
Same batch, same fix window. Past ~155–160 characters Google usually rewrites the snippet itself,
which means these guides lose control of their own SERP pitch. Worst offenders:

| Length | Page |
|---|---|
| 233 | `/guides/autumn-planting-garlic-onions` |
| 213 | `/guides/growing-root-vegetables` |
| 211 | `/guides/growing-veg-in-containers` |
| 209 | `/guides/protecting-vegetables-from-frost` |
| 207 | `/guides/succession-sowing` |

Trim each to end on a complete, punchy sentence at ~150 characters rather than running a second
sentence past the cut-off.

### 5. Housekeeping, bundle into the pass above rather than doing separately
- **54 pages** still carry a `keywords` meta array — `CUT_LIST.md` already flagged this as cargo-cult
  (Google has ignored the tag since 2009). Harmless, but since you'll be in most of these `/guides`
  files anyway for #3/#4, worth deleting the array while you're there.
- **Homepage has two `<h1>` elements** — identical text ("Know exactly what to plant, right now"),
  one wrapped `sm:hidden` (mobile) and one `hidden sm:flex` (desktop). Both sit in the DOM regardless
  of viewport, just CSS-toggled — a common responsive pattern, and since the text is identical (not
  two different headings competing, which is the real ANTI_PATTERNS "stutter" problem) the SEO impact
  is minimal. Low priority; only worth folding into one landmark if you're touching the homepage
  template anyway.

## OPPORTUNITIES (current UK season: early September)

1. **Leaf mould making.** Real, current search demand — competitor sites (e.g. gardenuk.co.uk) run
   dedicated "how to make leaf mould" pages, and September is peak leaf-fall collection time. What To
   Sow only mentions leaf mould in passing inside `/guides/soil` and `/guides/pests` — no dedicated
   coverage. Natural home: a proper section in `/guides/preparing-your-plot-for-winter`, which already
   covers "mulching beds the no-dig way" — leaf mould is the free version of exactly that, not a new
   standalone page.
2. **What to do with green tomatoes as the season ends.** Well-evidenced September search topic —
   many dedicated UK gardening articles on ripening green tomatoes indoors and cooking with them.
   `/guides/tomato-blight` and `/guides/growing-tomatoes-outdoors-vs-greenhouse` don't cover the
   end-of-season ripening problem at all. Fits either guide as a new section, or — given the site's
   real-plot voice — a timely diary/blog post written as it's happening.
3. **Storing apples.** UK harvest/storage searches run through September–November with strong
   competitor coverage (Gardeners' World, Garden Ninja and others all have dedicated pages).
   `/guides/growing-fruit` covers growing fruit but has zero mention of storage or curing — a
   near-miss worth a section addition rather than a new page.
