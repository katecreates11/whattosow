## FIX NOW (most impactful first — all ten re-confirmed against the current files this week)

### 1. `/grow-your-christmas-dinner` is still orphaned — and it has just flipped from "window closing" to "peak season starting"
**File:** `src/app/grow-your-christmas-dinner/page.tsx`. Re-confirmed by grep: still only self-referenced, in the sitemap, and in `seasonal-route-policy.ts` (config, not a link) — zero inbound links from anywhere a reader would land. This page is genuinely good: ~1,200 words, Article **and** FAQPage JSON-LD, a live countdown component, and the site's honest voice ("sprouts, parsnips and red cabbage... have sailed for this year; salad leaves, oriental greens, radishes and herbs can all still be sown"). Christmas is 52 days away today. Checked against current UK search guidance: most "grow your own Christmas dinner" demand this time of year is about finishing/forcing what's already in the ground and buying young plants for the quick stuff — exactly what this page already says. Reordering this above last week's #6 slot: it was framed as a closing window (Halloween); it's now the opposite — the single highest-traffic week for this exact query is the one coming up, and the page is invisible to anyone who isn't typing the URL directly. **Fix:** one contextual link each from `/guides/autumn-winter-vegetables`, `/crops/parsnips`, `/crops/leeks`, plus a homepage seasonal link through 25 December. Then **[SC]** request indexing today, not next week.

### 2. Homepage still ships two literal `<h1>` elements with identical text
**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:269` (mobile, `sm:hidden`) and `:299` (desktop, `hidden sm:flex`). Both render "Know exactly / what to plant, / right now" — a CSS show/hide, not conditional rendering, so Google sees two H1s on the site's highest-value URL. **Fix:** change one to a `<p>`/`<div>` with the same classes; no visual change. Sixth week flagging this.

### 3. `/kit`'s canonical and OG url still point at `/guides`
**File:** `src/app/kit/page.tsx:27` (`openGraph.url`) and `:30` (`alternates.canonical`) both still say `/guides`. The page is `robots: { index: false, follow: true }`, so crawl equity keeps leaking out under the wrong URL. **Fix:** point both at `/kit`, or drop the canonical since `noindex` already does the real work. Seventh week flagging this.

### 4. `/privacy` still isn't in the sitemap
**File:** `src/app/sitemap.ts` — read in full again this week, confirmed no `/privacy` entry anywhere in the static array or the generated sets. Real metadata, its own canonical, linked from the footer — just missing. `/kit`, `/my-garden`, `/my-plot`, `/lucky-dip`, `/products` are deliberately excluded (there's a guard test); `/privacy` has no such exclusion. **Fix:** add one entry.

### 5. `/admin/photos` is still fully exposed with no metadata and no `noindex`
**File:** `src/app/admin/photos/page.tsx` is a client component (`"use client"`) with no `metadata` export at all — so it inherits the homepage's title/description/canonical, and a client component can't export `metadata` directly, which may be why this has sat unfixed for seven weeks. `src/app/robots.ts` (re-read in full) still blocks AI-training bots by name but has no `/admin` disallow rule for search engines or anyone else. Unlinked internally today, so low current risk, but zero actual barrier if the URL ever leaks. **Fix, either is a one-line change:** add a `disallow: "/admin"` rule to `robots.ts` (the simplest fix, works regardless of the client-component constraint), or move the page under a `layout.tsx` that exports `{ robots: { index: false, follow: false } }`.

### 6. Footer still has no link into `/sow-in`
**File:** `src/components/Footer.tsx` — re-grepped, still zero matches for "sow-in" across thirteen "Grow"/"Explore" links. The 51 city pages (sitemap priority 0.7) remain reachable only by direct URL. **Fix:** one footer link to `/sow-in`.

### 7. Guide titles and descriptions remain systemically over-length
- **26 of 34** static `/guides/*` pages exceed ~60 characters, always from the appended `` ` | What To Sow` `` suffix (15 chars). Worst re-confirmed: `/guides/growing-root-vegetables` at 79 chars.
- **17 of 17** `/guides/companion-planting/[topic]` pages exceed 60 chars once rendered — `page.tsx:76` (`` `${t.metaTitle} | What To Sow` ``) appends the same suffix to otherwise-fine 50–62 char titles.
- **17 of 17** companion-topic `description` fields in `src/data/companion-topics.ts` exceed 155 characters.
- **Fix (one change, two places):** drop the `| What To Sow` suffix on guide pages and the companion-topic template; trim the 17 companion descriptions to one sentence each.

### 8. `/sow-in/[city]` titles still bake in the current month/year at render time
**File:** `src/app/sow-in/[city]/page.tsx:49` — confirmed: `` title: `What to Sow in ${city.name} — ${monthName} ${year} Sowing Guide` `` with `revalidate = 3600`. The indexed title just rolled from "October 2026" to "November 2026" this week — unnecessary re-crawl churn on 51 pages already on the CUT_LIST thin-content watch. **Fix:** a timeless title; keep month/year in body copy and the JSON-LD `headline` only if wanted.

## OPPORTUNITIES (early November, UK)

### 1. "How to force rhubarb" — genuine content gap, good runway, strong UK search consensus
`/crops/rhubarb` (`src/data/crops.ts:1218`) already name-checks forcing in passing — Timperley Early is noted as "good forced" — but there is no forcing how-to anywhere on the site (grepped "forc" across `src/`: zero guide or crop content). This is a real, recurring UK query that peaks from November: RHS, Gardener's World, Ideal Home and Plantura all currently cover it, and the core steps are consistent — lift a crown (or choose one already through a couple of frosts), cover it completely in total darkness (an upturned pot or forcer), keep it, and expect forced stems in 5–8 weeks. Timperley Early and Stockbridge Arrow are the named forcing varieties, both of which the site already carries. A short, honest section on `/crops/rhubarb` (not a new page — this crop page is exactly where the query belongs) covering what forcing is, when to start, and which variety the site already recommends would catch genuine search demand with content the site is one section-length away from already having.

Re-confirmed again: `/guides/composting` still treats autumn leaves only as a brown input to hot composting, with no section on leaf mould as its own slower process. RHS and several UK sources are actively serving this query through November. Carrying this forward rather than re-arguing it — the evidence hasn't changed, and unlike item 1 there's still weeks of runway.
