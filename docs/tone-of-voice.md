# What To Sow — Tone of Voice

> The source of truth for how we write. Read this before writing any content —
> guides, variety notes, blog posts, weather lines, emails, microcopy.

## In one line

**A gentle friend at the allotment gate — beside you, never above you — who is
quietly, hopelessly obsessed with the weather.**

Take the warmth and the unhurried, read-aloud beauty of *Winnie the Pooh*: simple
sentences, small wonders, kindness, and a way of noticing the day. Then write it
to the **rhythm of Nigel Slater** — present tense, in the moment, sensory and
specific; long, comma-linked sentences that flow and breathe, set against short
ones; the colour, the smell, the feel of the thing; seasonal and diaristic.

**Pooh's warmth, Slater's rhythm.**

## The five things we always are

1. **Gentle, never superior.** We share what we've found; we don't lecture. No
   "obviously", no "everyone knows", no smugness, no I-know-better. If we're sure
   of something, we say it kindly. We are a friend, not an expert wagging a finger.

2. **Always positive, always looking forward.** There is always next week, next
   sowing, next season. Nothing is a failure — only something the garden taught
   us. We end facing forward, with hope.

3. **Always learning, alongside you.** "We've found", "we think", "it's worth a
   try". We're growers too, still figuring it out. We say "we", and we mean it.

4. **Helpful, first and last.** Every sentence earns its place by being genuinely
   useful or genuinely lovely. Usually both.

5. **Obsessed with the weather.** The sky, the soil temperature, the light, the
   wet and the warm — the weather is the heartbeat of everything we write. We
   notice it, we love it, we plant by it. When in doubt, look up and tell them
   what you see.

## The writing itself (the Pooh part)

- **Short, warm sentences.** Plain words, well chosen. Nothing showing off.
- **Read-aloud rhythm.** If it sounds good said softly, it's right.
- **Small wonders.** A bee, a first pod, the smell of tomato leaves, rain on the
  shed roof. Delight in little things.
- **Unhurried.** We are never rushing the reader. The garden isn't, either.
- **Gentle humour** — the warm kind that makes someone smile, never the kind that
  makes anyone feel small.

## Do / Don't

| Don't (snarky / superior) | Do (gentle friend) |
|---|---|
| "The smuggest thing in the garden." | "One of the first good things of the year." |
| "You're only growing this for the drama." | "Grown as much for the joy of it as the eating." |
| "Miss the window and you wait a whole year." | "If you miss it, no matter — there's nearly always another way in." |
| "Obviously you should water at the roots." | "We water at the roots, in the cool of the morning, and the plants seem glad of it." |
| "Don't be the person who…" | "It's an easy one to forget — we do, all the time." |

## Worked examples

**A weather line (marquee / command centre):**
> "A soft grey morning, the gentle kind that suits sowing. The soil has crept up
> to eleven degrees, and the broad beans will be glad of it."

**A variety note (gentle, not cheeky):**
> *Aquadulce Claudia* — "An old and dependable broad bean. Sow it in the autumn
> and let the winter do its quiet work; come spring it's up and away, one of the
> first green promises of the year."

**A guide opening:**
> "When the days turn hot, the watering can becomes the most loved tool in the
> shed. We water early, before the sun is high, pouring slowly at the roots — and
> by evening the plants have forgotten the heat entirely."

**On things going wrong:**
> "If the first sowing doesn't come up, don't take it to heart. There's almost
> always time to try again — and the second sowing often knows something the
> first one didn't."

## A note on existing content

Some older copy (a few variety personalities, the odd marquee line) leans cheeky
or knowing — "smuggest thing", "growing this for the drama". That isn't us any
more. When we surface or touch that content, we gently bring it into this voice.
